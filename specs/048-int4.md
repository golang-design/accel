# int4

**One thing:** halving the weights again, with a bound that is not the old one.

[#22](https://github.com/golang-design/accel/issues/22). At 27B parameters
`quant`'s int8 is 26.7 GiB, which does not fit a 24 GiB card and is most of a
32 GiB machine before the KV cache. int4 is 13.4 GiB, which does. **So this
decides whether a 27B-class model is reachable on one device at all**, which no
other open report does.

## 1. Asymmetric, and why that is the whole design

[027](027-quantization.md)'s int8 is **symmetric**: one scale per block, zero
maps to zero, `Int8Max` is 127 rather than 128 so the range has no value without
a counterpart. That is the right shape at 8 bits and the wrong one at 4.

A 4-bit code has sixteen values. Spending them symmetrically around zero means a
group whose weights all sit near one nonzero value wastes almost all of them.
So a group carries a **scale and a zero point**:

$$
s_g = \frac{\max_g - \min_g}{15},
\qquad
z_g = -\frac{\min_g}{s_g},
\qquad
w_i \approx (q_i - z_g)\, s_g,
\quad q_i \in [0, 15]
$$

which is exactly what AWQ and GPTQ publish, so the same representation reads the
ecosystem's checkpoints and gives a caller a format to quantize into. Those were
[#15](https://github.com/golang-design/accel/issues/15)'s two goals and this is
where they stop being separate.

```
q = 0   ->  (0 - z)s  = min          q = 15  ->  (15 - z)s = 15s + min = max
```

## 2. The group is 128, and the reason is not the tiling

`Int8Block` is 32 because the tiled GEMM steps K in sixteen: a *tiling* choice,
made when the metadata it implied was invisible. At 4 bits it is not:

| | bytes/weight | 27B | metadata, as a share of the payload |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| int8, fp16 scale per 32 | 1.0625 | 26.7 GiB | 6.2% |
| int4, fp16 scale per 32 | 0.5625 | 14.1 GiB | **12.5%** |
| int4, fp16 scale **and zero** per 128 | 0.53125 | 13.4 GiB | 6.2% |

**Halving the payload doubles the metadata's share of it.** A group of 128
carries *twice* as much per group and still costs less, because it is amortised
over four times as many weights. And 128 is a multiple of sixteen and is exactly
the row kernels' width, so the argument that fixed 32 **permits** 128 rather
than forbidding it.

## 3. The bound, derived like §3 of 027 and not equal to it

Rounding to nearest gives at most half a step, and the step here is a **range**
where int8's was a **peak**:

$$\big|w_i - (q_i - z_g)s_g\big| \;\le\; \frac{s_g}{2} \;=\; \frac{\max_g - \min_g}{30}$$

Per dot product of length $K$ against activations $x$:

$$\left|\sum_i (q_i - z_{g(i)})s_{g(i)} x_i - \sum_i w_i x_i\right|
\;\le\; \frac{1}{30}\sum_i |x_i| \left(\max_{g(i)} - \min_{g(i)}\right)$$

**Range-over-30 against peak-over-254 is what asymmetry buys and costs**, and
stating both is the point:

| weights in a group | int8 symmetric | int4 asymmetric |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| $[0.9, 1.0]$ | 3.94e-3 | **3.33e-3** |
| $[-1, 1]$ | **3.94e-3** | 6.67e-2 |

A group clustered away from zero is represented *better* by four asymmetric bits
than by eight symmetric ones, because the symmetric form spends its codes on a
range the weights never visit. A group centred on zero is 17× worse, and that is
the honest cost of the halving rather than a caveat.

**The accumulation term is unchanged.** Products are summed in f32, so
[008](008-numerics.md) §7's reduction bound applies as it does for int8, and the
budget is the two added — conservative, deliberately, because they are not
independent.

## 4. Packing, stated because a kernel and a quantizer must agree

Eight weights per **uint32**, low nibble first:

```
word j holds weights 8j .. 8j+7
  weight 8j+n  ->  bits 4n .. 4n+3
```

so `w[i]` is `(packed[i/8] >> (4*(i%8))) & 0xF` with no branch.

**Words rather than bytes, and that was a correction.** This section first said
two weights per byte, and the generator refused the kernel: *"arithmetic on u8
is outside the subset: narrow dtypes are storage and convert to f32 on load"*.
[002](002-compute-model.md) makes that rule, and a shift and a mask are
arithmetic — so a byte-packed format is one a kernel cannot unpack at all.

The byte form would have needed the caller to reinterpret the slice as words at
upload, which works only because every supported platform is little-endian.
Packing into words removes that dependency rather than relying on it, and it is
also what AWQ and GPTQ pack into, so the ecosystem's files need no repacking.

A group of 128 weights is 16 words, so a group boundary is always a word
boundary and no weight straddles one.

## 5. What is built, and what is not

**Built:** the representation in `quant`, its bound, and a matvec kernel that
reads it.

**Not built: reading a published AWQ or GPTQ file.** The *representation* is
theirs; the file format, its permutation of columns, and its packing order are
not, and each is a separate piece of work with its own failure mode. What this
spec buys is that when that is built, the arithmetic underneath it already
exists and is bounded. [#15](https://github.com/golang-design/accel/issues/15)
remains the row for the file formats.

**Built as of 2026-08-27: the tiled int4 GEMM.** See §5.2.

### 5.2 The prefill shape — 2026-08-27

The matvec is what a decode step needs, which is where the memory pressure this
spec exists for is felt. A prefill has many rows against one matrix, and reading
the matrix once per token wastes the bandwidth the format exists to save.

`QuantMatMulInt4` is [010](010-kernel-corpus.md)'s `matmul_tiled_f32` with one
change, and the change is the reason it is a kernel rather than a wrapper: **the
unpacking moves from the inner loop to the tile load.**

```
             per use                        once per tile
matvec:  word ─▶ nibble ─▶ (q−z)·s ─▶ ×
matmul:  word ─▶ nibble ─▶ (q−z)·s ─▶ tileB ─▶ TileM reads ─▶ ×
```

$$
\text{out}[m][n] = \sum_k a[m][k]\cdot\big(\text{code}(kN{+}n) - z_g\big)s_g,
\qquad g = \lfloor (kN{+}n)/G \rfloor
$$

The arithmetic is unchanged, so §3's bound covers this kernel without
restatement. What changes is that each weight is decoded once and read `TileM`
times instead of decoded on every use, which is the whole difference between the
two shapes.

**The nibble order is now spelled in four places** — the quantizer, the
unpacker, the matvec and this. That is one more chance to disagree, so the
assertion in §6 is a differential against the matvec rather than a golden: two
implementations that agree with each other about the wrong order round-trip
perfectly, and a third reading the same buffer does not.

**Still not built: reading a published AWQ or GPTQ file**, unchanged above.


## 5.1 Built — 2026-08-26

§1's representation, §3's bound, §4's packing, and a matvec kernel. Every
assertion in §6 is built.

**The bound holds with no slack**, which is a design choice rather than luck:
the quantizer stores the scale and the zero point as f16 and reads them *back*
before quantizing against them, so the reconstruction uses the same two numbers
the quantizer used and the only error left is rounding the code. I wrote the
test with slack for their f16 rounding first and removed it after checking,
which is the right order to discover that in.

**Measured, and the table in §3 holds.** On weights in $[0.9, 1.0]$ the 4-bit
asymmetric form is off by 3.21e-3 and the 8-bit symmetric one by 3.87e-3 — four
bits beating eight, because the symmetric form spends its codes on a range those
weights never visit. Both directions are asserted, so the cost of the halving is
a fact this package states about itself rather than a caveat in a document.

**The nibble order is checked by a third implementation.** A packer and an
unpacker that agree with each other about the wrong order round-trip perfectly;
only a kernel reading real weights notices. Reversing the order inside the word
gives a product of 11.7 where the answer is 40, and the test names both.

## 6. Done

Each assertion names the mutation it catches.

- **A group of constant weights reconstructs exactly.** $\max = \min$ makes the
  scale zero, which is the division a naive quantizer performs and the NaN it
  produces. The reconstruction is the constant, not zero.
- **The reconstruction is within the derived bound**, checked per weight against
  $(\max_g - \min_g)/30$ over random groups — not against a tolerance somebody
  tuned.
- **A group clustered away from zero beats int8 on the same weights**, which is
  §3's table asserted rather than described, and which fails for a symmetric
  4-bit form.
- **The packing round-trips at an odd length**, where the final byte holds one
  weight and half a nibble of nothing.
- **The kernel and the quantizer agree about which nibble is which**, checked by
  a dot product rather than by unpacking — swapping them still round-trips
  through the packer alone.
- **CPU and Metal agree** on the matvec, exactly: the arithmetic is integer
  unpacking, two f32 multiplies and a sum, and [008](008-numerics.md) §7's
  reduction bound is the only slack.
- **The tiled kernel and the row kernel agree, row for row**, within
  [008](008-numerics.md) §7's reduction bound. This is what checks the fourth
  spelling of the nibble order against the third; a golden would check it
  against itself.
- **The batched operator refuses a vector by name**, pointing at `Int4MatVec`,
  so a caller who wrote the slower shape is told which one they wrote.
